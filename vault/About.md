I’m a physics student and recreational mathematician with a particular interest in connections between ideas that initially seem unrelated. Much of my attention goes to differential geometry and linear algebra, with category theory becoming a growing part of that exploration.

My interest in physics began at nine, when a NOVA documentary left me wanting to study the subject. Once I reached university, concern about my mathematical preparation pushed me to build a stronger foundation for my courses; over time, that practical motivation developed into an appreciation for mathematics in its own right. Physics continues to give me a place to apply what I learn, while mathematics has become the subject I most enjoy pursuing for its own sake.

Early in my undergraduate studies, I noticed that the formula for the reciprocal of a complex number resembled the inverse formula for a real 2 × 2 matrix. Following that resemblance led me to work out a representation of the complex numbers by real matrices, with addition and multiplication preserved. Finding a structural connection behind a familiar calculation is the kind of experience that keeps drawing me further into the subject.

I write both to share what I find interesting and to give myself explanations I can return to: the better a teaching tool I make now, the easier it will be to relearn an idea later. My aim is to develop rigorous arguments in a conversational voice, giving the notation and the accompanying explanations the same care. I believe it is possible to be exact without being obtuse, and I write with motivated undergraduates in mind, whether they are filling a gap in their understanding or looking for a more approachable route into a rigorous treatment.

I’m also contributing as a category-theory consultant to an early-stage paper exploring possible extensions of fusion categories. The project is motivated by their relationship with topological quantum field theory, bringing my growing interest in categorical structures into contact with my background in physics.
