I’m a physics student and hobbyist mathematician interested in the more abstract side of things. Within mathematics, my main areas of interest include differential geometry and linear algebra, with category theory taking up more of my focus recently.

I originally became interested in physics at nine, when a NOVA documentary on black holes led to me reading every book I could find on them in my public library. Once I reached university, concern about my mathematical preparation pushed me to build a stronger foundation for my courses; over time, that practical motivation developed into an appreciation of math for math's sake. While I still consider myself a physicist in temperament, it has shifted from my main priority to an area in which I can apply any fun math I learn.

My interest in mathematical exposition began early in my undergraduate studies. While reading a complex analysis textbook, I noticed that the formula for the multiplicative inverse of a complex number resembled the inverse formula for a real 2 × 2 matrix. Pulling on that thread led to me independently developing several aspects of representation theory before having encountered it formally. Realizing I would likely lose the intuition I had built from this exercise over time, I opted to do a small write-up which ended up being shared around the department, helping to crystallize my understanding and share it with others.

Today I write both to share what I find interesting and to give myself explanations I can return to: the better a teaching tool I make now, the easier it will be to relearn an idea later. My aim is to develop rigorous arguments in a mostly conversational voice, using rigorous notation and semantics while providing the motivation behind every construction where possible. I believe it is possible to be formal without being obtuse, and I write with the motivated undergraduate in mind.

Recently, on the professional side, I’ve also begun early work on a paper examining how the assumptions placed on categories affect the topological quantum field theories we can construct from them. Fusion categories with suitable additional structure provide a starting point, with examples including the Turaev–Viro construction from spherical fusion categories. I’m interested in what remains possible when some of those assumptions are relaxed, and whether the resulting theories can be classified in terms of the categorical structures used to construct them. My contribution is primarily on the category theory side, but I expect to become more involved in the physical interpretations of these constructions as the work progresses.
