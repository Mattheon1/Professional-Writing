## Metric Topology on Affine Spaces
Given an $N$-dimensional real affine space $\mathcal{A}:=(A,\vec{A},+,-),$ choosing an ordered [[Affine Basis|affine basis]]$$B:=(p_{0},p_{1},\dots,p_{N})$$allows us to identify $\mathcal{A}$ with the affine space $(\mathbb{R}^N,\mathbb{R}^N,+,-)$ via an [[Affine and Barycentric Coordinates|affine coordinate system]]  $$\begin{align}
\phi_{p_{0}}&:A\to \vec{A};\qquad p\mapsto p-p_{0}, \\
\Phi_{\vec{B}}&:\vec{A}\to \mathbb{R}^N;\qquad (p_{i}-p_{0})\mapsto e_{i}, \\
\Psi:=\Phi_{\vec{B}}\circ\phi_{p_{0}}&:A\to \mathbb{R}^N.
\end{align}$$
As the Euclidean norm on $\mathbb{R}^N$ induces the standard metric topology $\tau_{\mathbb{R}^N}$ generated by the open balls $$B_{\epsilon}(x):=\{ y\in \mathbb{R}^N:\lVert x-y \rVert<\epsilon \},$$requiring $\Psi$ be a homeomorphism endows $A$ with the pullback topology
$$\tau_{A}:=\{ \Psi^{-1}(U):U\in \tau_{\mathbb{R}^N} \}.$$

Since $\Phi_{\vec{B}}$ is a linear isomorphism, the Euclidean norm induces a norm on $\vec{A}$ given by
$$
\lVert p-q\rVert_{\vec{B}}:=\lVert\Phi_{\vec{B}}(p-q)\rVert.
$$
As $\Phi_{\vec{B}}(p-q)=\Psi(p)-\Psi(q)$, the pullback topology $\tau_A$ may equivalently be categorized as the topology generated by the open balls
$$
B_{\epsilon}(p):=\{q\in A:\lVert p-q\rVert_{\vec{B}}<\epsilon\}.
$$

## Coordinate Independence

This choice of topology can be shown to be independent of the coordinate system, despite being explicitly constructed from one. Letting $$B:=(p_{0},p_{1},\dots,p_{N})$$and $$B':=(p'_{0},p'_{1},\dots,p'_{N})$$ be two affine bases, with corresponding vector-space coordinate maps

$$  
\begin{align}  
\Phi_{\vec{B}} &:\vec{A}\to\mathbb{R}^N,  
&\qquad \Phi_{\vec{B}'} &:\vec{A}\to\mathbb{R}^N,  
\end{align}  
$$

and affine coordinate maps

$$  
\begin{align}  
\Psi:A &\to\mathbb{R}^N,  
&\qquad \Psi':A &\to\mathbb{R}^N,  
\end{align}  
$$

we define the corresponding topologies as

$$  
\tau_{A}:=\{\Psi^{-1}(U):U\in\tau_{\mathbb{R}^N}\},\qquad  
\tau'_{A}:=\{\Psi'^{-1}(U):U\in\tau_{\mathbb{R}^N}\}.  
$$

Writing $\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\vec{B}}$ and $\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\vec{B}'}$ for the norms on induced by the respective bases, the open balls generating $\tau_A$ and $\tau'_A$ are given by
$$
B_{\epsilon}(p)=\{q\in A:\lVert p-q\rVert_{\vec{B}}<\epsilon\},\qquad
B'_{\epsilon}(p)=\{q\in A:\lVert p-q\rVert_{\vec{B}'}<\epsilon\}.
$$

Since $\vec{A}$ is finite-dimensional, these norms are equivalent. Thus there exist $m,M>0$ such that, for every $p,q\in A$,
$$
m\lVert p-q\rVert_{\vec{B}'}
\leq \lVert p-q\rVert_{\vec{B}}
\leq M\lVert p-q\rVert_{\vec{B}'}.
$$
Applying the upper and lower bounds, respectively, gives
$$
B'_{\epsilon/M}(p)\subseteq B_{\epsilon}(p),
\qquad
B_{m\epsilon}(p)\subseteq B'_{\epsilon}(p)
$$
for every $p\in A$ and $\epsilon>0$. 

As both topologies are generated by their respective balls, given an arbitrary $U\in\tau_A$, for every $p\in U$ there exists some $\epsilon>0$ such that $$B'_{\epsilon/M}(p)\subseteq B_{\epsilon}(p)\subseteq U.$$
Thus $\tau_{A}\subset \tau'_{A}$. Conversely for arbitrary $U'\in \tau'_{A},$ every point $p\in U'$ admits an $\epsilon>0$ such that
$$B_{m\epsilon}(p)\subseteq B'_{\epsilon}(p)\subseteq U',$$implying $\tau'_{A}\subseteq \tau_{A}.$

Hence $\tau_A=\tau'_A$ and we may refer unambiguously to the standard metric topology on an affine space.
